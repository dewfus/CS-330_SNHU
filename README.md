# CS-330 Computational Graphics and Visualization — Final Project Reflection

## How do I approach designing software?

This project added a spatial dimension to how I think about design that I had not really exercised before. The core skill was learning to look at a real object and mentally break it down into geometric primitives before writing any code. The process I followed was staged: get the shapes right first, then add textures, then add lighting. I never moved to the next layer until the current one was stable. That approach kept the problem manageable and meant I always had something working to build on. I think that same principle applies to most software projects and is something I will carry forward.

## How do I approach developing programs?

The biggest shift this project pushed me toward was modularity. Breaking the scene into individual render functions for each object made debugging straightforward and kept the codebase readable throughout. Iteration was central to the whole process, and what evolved across the milestones was my confidence in making large adjustments rather than small cautious ones. Early on I was hesitant to change something that was already working. By the end I had enough understanding of the system to make bigger changes and predict roughly what effect they would have. That shift from cautious to confident iteration is probably the most practical thing I took away from this project.

## How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

My background is in Mass Communication and I am pursuing Computer Science as a second degree. This course filled in a part of the computing stack I had always treated as a black box. Understanding how 3D scenes are constructed, how lighting shapes the perception of objects, and how camera perspective influences what a viewer takes away from an image gives me a more informed foundation for working with visual media in any context. Every application with a graphical interface, every game, every simulation is running some version of what I built here, and having worked through the fundamentals by hand means I can engage with higher level tools in those areas with a much clearer picture of what is actually happening beneath the surface.
